# Owning the frame: the 2D part (branch frame-2d)

The screen's 2D drawn by HiFi XI instead of the game: the whole interface (every sprite part, text glyph, icon, the
credits backdrop, the radar, the mouse cursor, the viewer marker), the fade after the menus, the screen-text queue, and
on A the wash and the screenshot's copyright text, with the pattern of PLAN-own-frame.md: a game-side recorder, slot
markers, the Direct3D 12 draw at the markers, a gate that leaves it all to the game until HiFi XI's side has shown it
can draw it. Sources: research/ffxi-re/notes-screen-passes.md, notes-ui-draw.md, notes-original-renderer.md,
client-notes.md ("Frame order, complete", "Fonts"). R = read in the dump, C = seen in a capture or against dgVoodoo, I =
inferred.

**Nothing here has been seen in game.** It is tested offline: both FFXiMain dumps, the five d3dlog captures, dgVoodoo's
own d3d8.dll (drawn by WARP) as the reference, Direct3D 12 WARP with the debug layer. It is **off by default**:
`/hifi ui on` (or `[screen2d] native = 1`), `/hifi ui shadow` records and checks without drawing, `/hifi ui off`.

## The approach: record at the device, not per drawer

notes-ui-draw offers two recording levels. Function level (stubs at 11F300, 11BBD0, 026990/026AD0/026C70, 163400)
knows what each drawer means but not the device state around it: the drawers leak state into each other (11E980 sets
ADDRESSU/V WRAP around a part and CLAMP after, which the next 11EF60 tile then uses; 026990 sets only ALPHABLENDENABLE
and keeps whatever factors the last draw left; the text object sets no texture or blend in 11BBD0). Fidelity needs the
device's real state per draw, so the recorder works at the device:

- **The window.** Two windows a frame, opened by patched calls in the loop tail (011A00, R, both dumps):

  | Window | Opens | Cuts / ends | Marker kinds |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | the back buffer | after the resolve's Z states: `012779 call 00BB20` (an empty `ret`) | `0129EA call 00A820` (before the fade after the menus: the interface's end), `012AF5 call 009740` (EndScene: closed) | 9 begin, 10 interface end and cuts, 11 end |
  | A (the screen passes) | `012465 call 012F40` (before the frozen-frame crossfade) | `0126A1 call 00A1D0` (A popped: closed) | 8 cuts and end |

  Everything the game draws in the back buffer's window is 2D: radar (15FA00), menus (15F6A0), viewer marker
  (1D5680), the UI-size target's copy and stretch (0127A7, 00ABE0), the fade (00A820), the movie (21D4C0), the
  screen-text queue (038520). In A's: the crossfade (012F40), the wash and afterimage (0132B0), the copyright text
  (1D0B00), the UI states.
- **The device.** While a window is open the client device (Ashita's object, `[[0x45666C]+0xC]`) wears a copy of its
  own vtable (slot -1 and IDirect3DDevice8's 97 methods as it has them when the window opens: Ashita's, or hifps's draw
  tap). Our slots: SetRenderState, SetTextureStageState, SetTexture, SetViewport, SetVertexShader, SetPixelShader,
  ApplyStateBlock (the state read again with the Get methods after it), SetRenderTarget, Clear, DrawPrimitive,
  DrawIndexedPrimitive, DrawPrimitiveUP, DrawIndexedPrimitiveUP. Every state call is followed and then made on the
  device (so Direct3D 8's state is always the game's). The window's start reads the state with the Get methods (the
  resolve's leftovers). The vtable pointer goes back when the window closes; Ashita's own ImGui and fonts draw later, in
  the present path, on Ashita's own device: they stay over everything.
- **What's recorded.** A DrawPrimitiveUP of FVF 0x144 or 0x44 (XYZRHW, D3DCOLOR, one UV or none), triangles, with Z,
  fog and stencil off, solid fill, no pixel shader, stage 1 off, stage 0's ops among DISABLE..BLENDCURRENTALPHA with
  DIFFUSE/CURRENT/TEXTURE/TFACTOR/SPECULAR (+ COMPLEMENT, ALPHAREPLICATE), POINT/LINEAR filters, WRAP/MIRROR/CLAMP, a
  managed texture of a format the store takes (DXT1-5, 8888, X888, 565, 1555, 4444, A8, L8, A8L8). Strips and fans are
  unrolled into a list with each triangle's first vertex first (the flat-shaded colour). The draw keeps Direct3D 8's
  values: blend (enable, factors, op), write mask, cull, the combiner, alpha test, shade mode, sampler, texture factor,
  the viewport and the target's size.
- **What's the game's.** Anything else (a DrawPrimitive/DIP/DIPUP, a render-target or dynamic texture, another FVF,
  fog, Z...) **cuts**: what's recorded so far is published as a segment and its marker sent, then the game's call goes
  through. A Clear and a SetRenderTarget cut the same way. So the order on screen is the game's whatever is left to it:
  the crossfade's and afterimage's copies of A, the UI-size target's stretch, the resolve.
- **Native or not.** With the gate native, the recorded DrawPrimitiveUPs are not made on the device (dgVoodoo never sees
  them); in shadow they are, and the Direct3D 12 side only checks it could have drawn each segment.
- **Textures** are taken from the game's device the first time a draw samples one: GetLevelDesc (a managed texture
  only), LockRect read only, every level; handed to the store (src/ui2d/render2d), which uploads them before the marker
  that needs them (one wait a flush, on our own queue). The texture is held (AddRef) while the store has it, so it
  can't be freed and another made at its address; its pixels are checked again (all of a small one, the first window
  it's used in; 16 rows of a large one every 180 windows) and taken again when they changed; let go after 300 windows
  unused. A draw that samples no texture keeps none.
- **The opening movie** (21D4C0 draws a texture its PMV decoder writes every frame) is left to the game behind a gate: no
  window opens while the movie player `[0x456D1C]` exists (01298E, R). The PMV codec isn't decoded.

## The markers and where they draw

One-pixel stencil-only clears of the **bound** depth through the client device, as zoneslot's and charslot's: x = 16
kind + (seq & 15), row 0. The back buffer has its own D24S8 (the d3dlog captures: `SetRenderTarget 1EB1F1CC z
1EB1F474 2560x1440 D24S8` at 0126A6), so the back buffer's markers need nothing bound briefly; A's are on A's own
depth. A window opens only while the bound depth has a stencil, and a SetRenderTarget in the window to a target whose
depth has none leaves the rest of it to the game (its segment flushed before the switch, on the good depth).

- Kind 9 starts the back buffer's window: the Direct3D 12 side counts its segments from there. A's window has no
  begin marker: its seq, new, starts the count.
- Each 10, 11 or 8 draws the window's next segment into **the target dgVoodoo bound with the marker's depth**
  (`passes::world::bound_rt`, `bound_ds`; dgVoodoo binds the D3D8 target pair including its depth even with Z off:
  dgvprobe's replay log). dgVoodoo binds lazily, so when the bound depth isn't the marker's the target last bound with
  it is used (remembered per depth).
- A's state: as the world's barriers left it on the list (`world::rt_state`); made a render target for our draws and put
  back. The back buffer is a target throughout.
- **The upscaled world first.** With FSR the upscale waits for the next call on the list after the resolve; a marker
  isn't a clear to it. The backend now lets it in before dispatching a back-buffer marker, so the UI lands over the
  upscaled world (and over the world part's names drawn as the upscale's overlay).

## The 2D renderer (src/ui2d/render2d)

Matched against dgVoodoo 2.87's own d3d8.dll, the game's dgVoodoo.conf (tests/ui2d_dgv.cpp):
- **Placement.** Pre-transformed vertices placed in a Direct3D 12 viewport of the Direct3D 8 one moved by half a pixel
  (as dgvprobe's log shows dgVoodoo set it: `RSSetViewports 16.5 1225.5 980 195` for the chat log), NDC from the
  vertex's distance to the viewport's corner, w = 1 / rhw (perspective-correct); clipped by the viewport, the scissor
  the whole target. SV_VertexID starts at 0 on WARP whatever the start vertex: the draw's first vertex is a constant.
- **Combiner.** Stage 0 as Direct3D 8: the ops saturate; COLOROP DISABLE = the diffuse colour and alpha. **A stage that
  samples TEXTURE with no texture set is drawn as disabled by dgVoodoo** (C: SELECTARG1, ADD and MODULATE2X of TEXTURE
  without one all give the diffuse colour and alpha), so the recorder records it that way.
- **Alpha test** in float (alpha against ref / 255), as dgVoodoo keeps the faint edge texels a rounding would drop.
- **Blend.** One pipeline per (enable, factors, op, write mask, cull, target format): Direct3D 8's one factor pair for
  colour and alpha (a colour factor read as its alpha on the alpha channel; BOTHSRCALPHA/BOTHINVSRCALPHA split),
  ADD/SUBTRACT/REVSUBTRACT/MIN/MAX. The PS2 table of 11F300 comes out as the game sets it (REVSUBTRACT shadows under
  labels, ONE/SRCALPHA SUBTRACT, DESTCOLOR/SRCALPHA, ZERO/INVSRCALPHA...). Cull: Direct3D 8's counter-clockwise =
  Direct3D 12's back with clockwise fronts.
- **Filtering as dgVoodoo forces it.** The game's dgVoodoo.conf says `Filtering = 1` (anisotropic at level 1) and
  `KeepFilterIfPointSampled = true`. C: dgVoodoo then samples **MAG POINT + MIN LINEAR (every UI sprite) as
  anisotropic too**, i.e. bilinear when magnified; only MINFILTER POINT keeps the game's filter. The plugin reads
  `[DirectX] Filtering` and `KeepFilterIfPointSampled` from the dgVoodoo.conf beside the loaded d3d8.dll (read only) and
  samples the same way (`render::filtering`; appdriven, pointsampled, bilinear, pointmip, linearmip, trilinear, 1-16).
- **Textures**: DXT1/2-3/4-5 uploaded as BC1/BC2/BC3 (the same blocks dgVoodoo uploads), 8888 and X888 as they are, the
  rest expanded to BGRA8 (and DXT whose top level isn't whole blocks decoded: `texfmt::decode_blocks`). Every level; a
  sampler with MaxLOD 0 for MIPFILTER NONE.
- **State put back.** Our own heaps (4,097 SRVs, 126 samplers), root signature, pipelines, viewport, scissor, target;
  then dgVoodoo's heaps (as passes tracks them per list) and its state (`grass::list_state::restore`). No input
  assembler (vertices from a root SRV of our own ring): dgVoodoo's vertex buffers stay bound.
- Consecutive draws with the same state and contiguous vertices are one Direct3D 12 draw; nothing is reordered.
- Pipelines are made on the game's thread when a draw needs one (the target's format learned from the markers), never
  on dgVoodoo's thread in the normal course; a segment whose pipeline or texture isn't there isn't drawn at all (and
  the gate closes).

## The gate (src/ui2d/gate.hpp, each present)

Off unless `[screen2d] native`, the five calls are ours and the renderer is made. Shadow (the game draws, every
segment checked) until the markers come through (a begin or a segment within 250 ms), 30 segments in a row could have
been drawn and the target is known; then native until a segment can't be drawn (2 s of shadow, then 30 in a row again)
or the markers stop for a quarter second. `[screen2d] shadow` never goes native. A frame recorded native in flight when
the Direct3D 12 side fails loses its 2D for that frame (the zone's and characters' gates have the same window).

## Shared files touched (small, additive)

- `src/host/passes.hpp/.cpp`: `world` gains `bound_rt`, `bound_ds`, `bound_rt_format` (what dgVoodoo last bound) and the
  list's `heaps`/`heap_count`; `passes::on_heaps` / `on_reset` track each list's heaps (a mutex; dispatch fills them).
  Any part that sets heaps of its own can use them.
- `src/backend/backend.cpp`: calls `passes::on_heaps` / `on_reset`; fills the bound target; for the back buffer's kinds

## Frame generation and the UI

The 2D is drawn on the back buffer after the upscale, at the screen's size, as the game drew it: nothing changes for
src/framegen, which takes FSR 2's picture as the frame without the UI and lets AMD's swap chain take the UI from the back
buffer where it differs. FSR 3's UI-composition path (a UI texture registered with the swap chain, composited over
real and generated frames) wants the UI as one premultiplied "over" layer: out = ui.rgb + scene x (1 - ui.a). Most of
FFXI's UI fits (alpha blend, additive text: alpha 0), but REVSUBTRACT shadows (scene - c x a), ONE/SRCALPHA SUBTRACT,
DESTCOLOR/SRCALPHA (scene x (1 + a)) and ZERO/SRCALPHA don't: drawing the segments into a layer would lose the label
shadows' darkening. Next step if wanted: draw each segment twice, once onto the back buffer (exact) and once into a
UI layer where the non-"over" blends are approximated (REVSUBTRACT as a darkening with alpha c x a), and register that
layer; compare on generated frames only.

## Open (with next steps)

1. **Crossfade and afterimage natively.** They read A as a texture (render-target textures): left to the game as cuts
   in A's window. Next: at A's window's kind-8 markers, read the registers at the tail (`[0x456910]` and `[0x45689C]`;
   sys+0x32C/+0x330 and `[0x456938]`), keep half-size history textures of our own in Direct3D 12, copy A (zone_rt) into
   them and blend as 012F40 / 0132B0 part 2 do (notes-screen-passes 2.3-2.4), then skip the game's 012F40 and 0132B0's
   second part (their calls are next to the window's opening call).
2. **The UI-size target sharp** (config 0x25/0x26 different from the window): today the UI is drawn natively into the
   game's UI target at the UI size (its draws cut at the pop to the back buffer) and the game stretches it (00ABE0,
   left to it). Next: when the draws' target is wrapper+0x1E0, draw the segments into a private target of the back
   buffer's size (the back buffer's pixels, alpha 0), blending alpha as Direct3D 8 does, and composite it over the back
   buffer with alpha > 0 at the interface's end (00ABE0's alpha test), leaving the game's stretch empty.
3. **Higher-resolution text.** On Alex's PC the text is already the 2048 x 4096 PNG (config 0x24 = 2). For the 1024
   fonts ('font moji' DXT3, PNG 0x9A77) the store could take the 2048 x 4096 PNG (file 0x9A78, ROM/272/121) instead:
   identify the font by its pixels against the DAT's (texture_slot, at capture), decode the PNG with WIC, check it
   against Python's zlib decode. It changes the glyphs' look to the 2048 font's: an option, not a default. The gain at
   the window's own size is antialiasing, not sharpness: the cells are 16 pixels either way.
4. **The opening movie** stays the game's (the PMV codec, 21D9B0.., isn't decoded).
5. **Character creation's Dancer** draws into A before the tail with its own stencil use: A's window opens after it,
   on A's depth, which the Dancer clears to 0 every frame; not checked against a capture of those screens.
6. **dgVoodoo's exact sub-texel position** at 2:1 (the last bits of filtered texels). Matching it would need dgVoodoo's
   own vertex maths; worth it only if a difference shows.
7. **Texture formats**: P8 and other paletted formats aren't taken (the game converts paletted DAT pictures to 8888,
   R); BORDER addressing and TEXCOORDINDEX other than 0 go to the game.
